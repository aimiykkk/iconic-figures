About this website

Why we chose iconic figures, how the website is designed and how to use it.

Introduction

Why this website exists

We chose the theme Influential or Iconic Figures because well-known people make history easier to remember. A person's story shows why events mattered.

We selected six figures from six countries and different fields: Leonardo da Vinci, Ada Lovelace, Marie Curie, Mahatma Gandhi, Tunku Abdul Rahman and Nelson Mandela. Together they cover art, computing, science, nonviolent resistance and the birth of nations, including Malaysia.

Common threads

What the six lives share

Ada Lovelace, Marie Curie, Gandhi, Tunku Abdul Rahman, Nelson Mandela

They pushed against limits

Ada Lovelace and Marie Curie worked in times that gave women few routes into mathematics and science. Gandhi, Tunku Abdul Rahman and Nelson Mandela each challenged a system of unequal rule.

Leonardo, Curie, Gandhi, Tunku Abdul Rahman

Their ideas crossed borders

Curie left Poland to study in France. Gandhi shaped his methods in South Africa before he led in India. Tunku Abdul Rahman studied in England before leading Malaya. Leonardo spent his last years in France.

Ada Lovelace, Leonardo, Gandhi, Nelson Mandela

The work outlived the person

Ada died at 36, yet her notes are still cited in the history of computing. Leonardo's notebooks are studied five centuries on. Gandhi and Mandela left later movements a method as well as a goal.
